Wave dispersion in the relaxed micromorphic model

I derived by hand the six coupled wave equations of the isotropic reduced relaxed micromorphic model, verified every term in Mathematica, and computed its dispersion curves and closed form cutoff frequencies.

The question

Classical Cauchy elasticity cannot describe the frequency bands of microstructured materials. The relaxed micromorphic model adds an independent micro distortion field P to the displacement u, so each material point can vibrate internally. The task was to derive the governing equations for plane waves, implement the model, and read the physics off its dispersion curves.

What I did

  • Derived by hand the symmetric, skew and trace parts of the relative distortion, all four stress tensors, and the two strong forms, giving six coupled PDEs in u1, u2, p11, p12, p21 and p22 for waves along x.
  • Implemented the 1D model in Wolfram Mathematica and checked all six strong forms term by term against the hand derivation.
  • Computed the dispersion relation for wavenumbers from 0 to 2, and solved the determinant at k = 0 symbolically for the cutoff frequencies.
  • Extended the analysis to 2D with a wave vector at angles from 0 to π/2.

Results

  • The spectrum has two acoustic branches that start at zero frequency, the macroscopic longitudinal and shear waves, and four optic branches that start at finite cutoffs, the internal microstructural modes.
  • The cutoffs have closed forms that depend only on the micro and inertia parameters. With the given parameters they are 1.45, 5.99 and 7.75 (a double root), and they match the intercepts of the plotted branches.
  • In 2D the curves are identical for every propagation angle, as expected for an isotropic model.

What I learned

Doing the tensor algebra by hand and then making the code agree with it, equation by equation, is slower than trusting the software but catches the errors that matter. The optic branches with finite cutoffs are the mechanism that lets such continuum models describe band gaps in metamaterials.
