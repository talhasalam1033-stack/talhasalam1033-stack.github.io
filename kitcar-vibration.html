Freelance analysis project

Vibration of a kit car space frame

A beam element model of a welded aluminium kit car space frame, used to find its resonances below 200 Hz and to show how the phasing of the engine mount forces decides which of them are excited.

The question

An engine feeds oscillating forces into the chassis at frequencies set by its speed. If those frequencies meet a resonance of the frame, vibration, noise and fatigue loading grow. Where are the resonances of this frame, what shapes do they have, and how strongly does each mount arrangement excite them?

What I did

  • Modelled the 2250 mm long welded frame with Nastran beam elements and two thin walled box sections (45 by 45 by 1.5 mm and 35 by 35 by 1.2 mm), generating the input deck with a Python script.
  • Checked the model before using it: total mass of 23.616 kg against the target, and six clean rigid body modes in a free-free run.
  • Computed the normal modes below 200 Hz, then the frequency response from the engine mounts to three points on the frame for single point, in-phase and out-of-phase mount forces.

  • Torsion and bending sit close together, typical of an open ladder type frame with little diagonal bracing in the floor.
  • In-phase mount forces excite the symmetric modes and suppress the 35 Hz torsion; out-of-phase forces do the opposite; a single point force excites both families.
  • An engine idling at 900 rpm gives a second order excitation at 30 Hz that reaches 35 Hz near 1050 rpm, so the antisymmetric fundamental mode lies inside the normal operating range and is driven by engine rocking.

What I learned

The useful output of a modal study is a design decision. Here it was to control the engine rocking motion through mount stiffness or a torque restraint, and to add diagonal floor bracing, which raises torsional stiffness far more than larger longitudinal rails.
