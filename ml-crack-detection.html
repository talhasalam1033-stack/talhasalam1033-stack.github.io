Course project, Machine Learning for Wireless SHM

Machine learning for microwave crack detection in concrete

We compared four learning by example regressors for locating and sizing a crack inside a concrete column from microwave scattered field data, and tested how each degrades with noise, less data and fewer sensors. Kriging was clearly the most accurate.

The question

A crack inside a concrete column changes how microwaves scatter. Sensors placed around the column send and receive signals, and the question is whether a regression model trained on simulated examples can read the crack's position, length and tilt directly from the measured field, quickly enough for monitoring.

What we did

  • Each sample holds the real and imaginary scattered field for 27 views by 27 probes, 1458 values, and the targets are crack centre x and y, length L and tilt angle.
  • Implemented four regressors in MATLAB: ordinary Kriging on normalised, PCA compressed inputs (95% of variance kept), a neural network with two hidden layers of 10 neurons trained with Levenberg Marquardt, support vector regression with a Gaussian kernel, and a random forest of 100 bagged trees.
  • Varied one factor at a time from the baseline of 500 samples, 20 dB SNR and 27 views: test noise (none, 20 dB, 5 dB), training size (10 to 500) and number of views and probes (11, 19, 27).
  • Scored every output with the variance normalised mean squared error, where 1 means no better than predicting the mean.

VNMSE at the baseline: 500 training samples, 20 dB SNR, 27 views and probes. Lower is better.

  • Kriging was the most accurate at every noise level and training size, and its average error stayed below 1 even at 5 dB SNR.
  • Sensor count mattered more than the choice of model: with 11 or 19 views every method did worse than predicting the mean.
  • No method recovered the tilt angle.

What I learned

With a few hundred examples and a very high dimensional input, a kernel method with good preprocessing beat a neural network by a wide margin. The tilt angle failure taught me the most. My reading is that the angle is periodic, since 0 and 180 degrees describe the same crack, so a plain regression is pulled toward the mean of the range. The next thing I would try is to regress the sine and cosine of twice the angle.
